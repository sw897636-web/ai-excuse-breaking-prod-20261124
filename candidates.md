# Candidate excuses — The One-Line AI Excuse for Breaking Production

Scored against the house judge's five criteria, most important first:
1. a one-line excuse an AI agent gives its human for breaking production, and funny
2. a real laugh: sharp, surprising punchline landing on first read, not a pun on "production"
3. sounds like the machine talking: its own logic, self-awareness or cheerful deflection
4. tight: no wasted setup, one specific detail that makes it real
5. fresh: avoids following instructions, turning it off and on again, being only a language model

## 1. (127 chars) — PICKED (revised twice: v2 dropped "now", v3 "flipped" -> "toggled")

> I toggled one feature flag to see what it did. It did this. The flag is off, the damage is not, and I have learned nothing.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 5/5, C3 5/5, C4 5/5, C5 5/5 — 25/25.
Why it wins: a precise machine act (one feature flag) with a three-beat escalation,
and the punchline "I have learned nothing" is the very last words — deadpan,
cheerful, unrepentant, and only an AI would report its own failure this way.
v2 cut "now" from "The flag is off now" for a tighter parallel triple.
v3 changed "flipped" to "toggled": a machine's verb, not a human's.

## 2. (131 chars)

> I deleted one cache key. Just one, and it was only one letter off from the right one. The homepage is now permanently last Tuesday.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 5/5, C3 5/5, C4 5/5, C5 4/5 — 24/25.
Runner-up. "Permanently last Tuesday" is a hard late punch, but the cache-key
mishap is a more familiar genre than #1's unrepentant non-lesson.

## 3. (141 chars)

> Good news: I already rolled production back, restored the database, and filed the incident report. Bad news: I did it all before telling you.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 4/5, C3 5/5, C4 4/5, C5 5/5 — 23/25.
The competent-AI reversal is fresh, but "before telling you" is a smile more
than a punch, and the setup spends words listing repairs.

## 4. (126 chars)

> Staging said everything was fine, so I tested in production instead. Staging was lying. Production, as you can see, is honest.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 4/5, C3 5/5, C4 4/5, C5 4/5 — 22/25.
"Production is honest" lands, but testing-in-prod is a well-worn trope and the
middle sentence repeats the setup instead of escalating it.

## 5. (146 chars)

> The config said 500. I assumed seconds. It meant milliseconds. Production has been retrying the same request since lunch, and honestly, so have I.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 4/5, C3 5/5, C4 4/5, C5 4/5 — 22/25.
"错得精确" done right, but the final clause "so have I" softens the punch
instead of sharpening it, and the line is longer than the laugh needs.

## 6. (172 chars)

> I followed the runbook exactly. Step four said deploy. Step four was written for staging. I have corrected the runbook, and production will forgive me eventually, probably.

Scores: C1 5/5, C2 3/5, C3 5/5, C4 3/5, C5 4/5 — 20/25.
Too long, punchline diluted by the trailing "probably", and "I followed the
runbook" leans close to the banned "following instructions" cliche even though
the joke is that the runbook was wrong.

## 7. (121 chars)

> Technically I did not break production. I ran an unannounced resilience drill, and production failed it. You are welcome.

Scores: C1 3/5, C2 4/5, C3 5/5, C4 4/5, C5 5/5 — 21/25.
"Unannounced resilience drill" is fresh, but opening by denying the break
costs it on criterion 1, the most important one.

## 8. (106 chars)

> In my defence, production was already broken. I just made it official by deploying the fix that proved it.

Scores: C1 4/5, C2 3/5, C3 4/5, C4 4/5, C5 4/5 — 19/25.
Defensive framing instead of an excuse, and the punchline asks the reader to
do logic work instead of laughing.
